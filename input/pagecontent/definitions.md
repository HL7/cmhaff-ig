Philosophically, the Mobile Health workgroup favors using terms that are commonly accepted in the consumer mobile space, in preference
to terms that are used only in the EHR space, because of the target user for these devices, who are consumers rather than clinicians. However, where terms are used differently in EHR vs consumer spaces, we take note of that, and acknowledge the various uses. This does not purport to be an exhaustive set of mobile health definitions, but terms are included only to provide clarity within cMHAFF. See **[British Standards Institution Publicly Available Specification (PAS) 277:2015 Health and wellness apps. Quality criteria across the life cycle. Code of practice](https://shop.bsigroup.com/products/health-and-wellness-apps-quality-criteria-across-the-life-cycle-code-of-practice)** which has a good set of Terms and Definitions (section 3). Definitions below that are taken from PAS277 are labeled (PAS).

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| **Alert** | A communication conveying critical information that demands an active response from the user. |
| **App** | A compact software program designed to run on a computing platform—typically a mobile device—that executes specific functions without managing the computer's core operating system. Within cMHAFF, this refers to software downloaded onto a user's device, potentially backed by external infrastructure like cloud storage or processing. |
| **App store** | An online platform for distributing software, commonly used for mobile applications (also referred to as an "app marketplace"). |
| **Assessment** | Within cMHAFF, an overarching term for evaluating a consumer mobile health application against framework standards. Methods range from publisher self-attestation to rigorous third-party testing, endorsement, or accreditation by an authorized body (whether mandatory or voluntary), though cMHAFF does not dictate a specific approach. |
| **Caregiver** | A paid or unpaid individual within someone's social circle who assists with their healthcare needs, frequently addressing challenges linked to aging, disability, illness, or mental health conditions. Under cMHAFF, a caregiver might operate a health app on behalf of another individual. |
| **Consumer** | An individual buying goods or services for personal use; specifically, within cMHAFF, the person obtaining the mobile application. |
| **Consumer mobile health app** | An application designed for individual consumers (who may or may not be patients) rather than clinical professionals. Per US FDA definitions, mobile apps run on smartphones and similar wireless devices, and may include attached hardware accessories or software-hardware combinations. While mobile *medical* apps qualify as regulated medical devices (or transform mobile platforms into them), consumers utilize both medical and general wellness apps to manage personal health, such as tracking daily calorie intake for weight maintenance. |
| **Detailed Clinical Model** | A structured information model (DCM) capturing granular, precise clinical knowledge suitable for diverse healthcare contexts. |
| **Developer (app)** | The individual(s) or team responsible for coding and building the application, who may or may not be the same entity as the app's Publisher or Sponsor. |
| **Directive (EU)** | A European Union legislative act requiring member states to accomplish a specific outcome while leaving the implementation methods up to each nation—unlike EU regulations, which take immediate legal effect without needing national implementing measures. |
| **EHR** | Electronic Health Record: a digital version of a patient's medical history maintained over time by a healthcare provider, encompassing key clinical data such as demographics, clinical notes, diagnoses, and medications. |
| **FDA** | Food and Drug Administration: a United States federal agency responsible for regulating medical devices, including software classified as medical device technology. |
| **Health and wellness app** | An application designed to support any dimension of physical, mental, or social well-being for the user or another subject of care. |
| **HIPAA** | Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act of 1996: US legislation directing the Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) to establish standards safeguarding health data privacy and security. HHS implemented this through the HIPAA Privacy Rule (protecting individually identifiable health information) and the HIPAA Security Rule (establishing national safeguards for electronic protected health information). |
| **Mobile medical app** | Software programs operating on smartphones or other wireless communication devices—including hardware accessories or combined hardware-software systems—that meet medical device criteria. |
| **Mobile platform** | Handheld commercial or open computing environments (with or without wireless capabilities), typically encompassing smartphones, tablets, and wearables like smartwatches. |
| **Notification** | An overarching category for user-facing informational messages. Alerts fall under this category, whereas standard (non-alert) notifications deliver information without requiring user interaction. |
| **Pairing** | The process of establishing a secure, trusted link between two devices—such as connecting a fitness tracker to a smartphone—similar to linking wireless headphones or car audio via Bluetooth. |
| **Personal data** | Any data connected to an identified or identifiable individual. |
| **PGHD** | Patient-Generated Health Data: health information created, recorded, or collected by patients, family members, or caregivers to manage a health issue. Unlike clinical data gathered during provider visits, PGHD is captured directly by patients, who also control how it is shared with healthcare providers and others. |
| **PHR** | Personal Health Record (sometimes called a "Personal Health Environment"): a digital tool enabling patients to securely store, manage, and access their personal health information in a private setting. |
| **Publisher (app)** | The person or organization responsible for distributing the application and making it accessible to end users. |
| **Sponsor (app)** | The person or organization initiating and backing the creation or deployment of an application—such as a hospital offering an app to its patients or a company providing one to its workforce. |
| **Subject of care or wellbeing** | The individual whose health, care, or well-being the application is intended to support. |
| **Syncing** | Synchronizing data or app versions across multiple devices so they hold identical information, much like keeping a contact list updated across a phone, tablet, and smartwatch. |
| **User** | The individual directly operating the app's interface, who may be either the subject of care or a proxy assisting them. A single app may support multiple subjects of care on one device, either sharing an account or using separate user profiles. |
